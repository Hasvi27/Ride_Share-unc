The team

Who's in the car?

Three students who have all wondered why nobody else was heading the same way. Here they are, in their own words.

Smruti Ganta

Junior, Data Science, minors in Business and Entrepreneurship

Not only did I have to pay full price to go to the airport alone during rush hour (which meant I had to pay surge pricing), but once I got there, I realized that there were so many UNC students that I could've shared a ride with. I could've saved money and met someone new. Rideshare is supposed to connect students to build a better transportation community at UNC.

Hasvi Muriki

Sophomore, Business and Data Science, minor in Entrepreneurship

I'm a UNC student working on Ride Share because I've felt this exact headache firsthand. Taking at least two trips to the airport this semester meant dealing with super early hours, feeling bad asking friends to make the drive, and watching rideshare prices shoot way up. Building a space where UNC students can spot who's already heading the same way and split a fare feels like a practical fix to a problem I run into myself.

Oscar

Junior, Political Science

I'm a junior studying political science, and I've spent more time than I'd like thinking about how students get around without a car. I kept seeing other students heading the same way with no easy way to know that. A board where people can post a trip and split the fare seems like a simple fix for something I've run into myself.
